Introduction

Poker hands are the foundation of the game. Every round ends with a comparison of hands, and the strongest combination wins. Learning hand rankings is the first step to understanding poker strategy, because every decision depends on how strong your cards are relative to others.

How Hands Are Evaluated

A poker hand consists of five cards. The strength of the hand depends on how rare or difficult that combination is to make. The rarer the hand, the stronger it is. Even if you don’t have a strong hand, you can still win by bluffing or by forcing other players to fold before the showdown.

Hand Rankings

Hand Example Description
Royal Flush
The strongest possible hand: A, K, Q, J, 10 all in the same suit. Extremely rare.
Straight Flush
Five consecutive cards of the same suit. Very strong and hard to beat.
Four of a Kind
Four cards with the same rank. Only beaten by straight flush or royal flush.
Full House
Three of a kind plus a pair. Strong and very common winning hand.
Flush
Five cards of the same suit, not in sequence.
Straight
Five consecutive cards of any suit.
Three of a Kind
Three cards of the same rank.
Two Pair
Two separate pairs in the same hand.
One Pair
Two cards of the same rank.
High Card
No combinations—your highest card decides the strength.
